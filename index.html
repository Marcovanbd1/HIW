Framing note

The missing layer in health systems isn’t technology.

It’s interpretation.

Across health and care we are investing rapidly in triage, automation, dashboards, messaging, AI documentation, and digital access.

But the biggest pressure multiplier isn’t a lack of tools.

It’s that people struggle to understand what to do next.

The structural problem

Most systems assume people arrive knowing what they need.

In reality they arrive with uncertainty, stress, partial understanding, and cognitive overload.

So navigation demand is being generated by the system itself.

What currently exists

Built everywhere

Directories

Service finders

Referral pathways

Triage tools

Still missing

Something that understands a person’s situation

Explains what matters

Reduces options

Shows a clear next step

This missing layer now has a name

Support Playlists

A Support Playlist is a structured set of next steps generated from a person’s situation, not from a search query.

Two entry points. One intelligence layer.

Community entry

Someone says what’s going on

The system interprets context

They receive a Support Playlist

Clinical entry

Consultation or discharge ends

Clinical context already known

A Support Playlist is generated

Why this matters now

Until recently, interpreting messy real‑world situations reliably wasn’t technically possible.

LLMs changed that.

For the first time, systems can translate human situations into clear next steps.

Where this sits in the system stack

Triage systems decide urgency.

Directories store services.

Booking systems schedule care.

Clinical systems hold records.

Support Playlists connect them to human understanding.

Why this is commercially viable

Most digital health products only create value when the full pathway is integrated.

Support Playlists create value immediately, even in fragmented systems.

They work before transformation is complete.

Strategic positioning

Support Playlists are not a directory.

Not a triage system.

Not a booking tool.

They are infrastructure for understanding.

The opportunity

The systems that scale across health economies won’t be those with the most technology.

They will be those that make navigation feel simple, obvious, and humane.

Conversation focus

Neighbourhood Signals shows what systems need.

Support Playlists shows how individuals move.

Together they form a new category: Navigation Intelligence.
